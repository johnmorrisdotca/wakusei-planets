## [0.77.3] - 2026-11-26

- **A card can print the orbit and the discovery of a dwarf planet or a minor body** (MODULES-1353). `minorFact` on `/fact-sheets` gives the semi-major axis, the perihelion and the sidereal period of the osculating ellipse the package already stores, for the dwarf planets besides Pluto and for Bennu, Eros, Vesta, Pallas, Chiron, Pholus, Halley and Arrokoth. `/discovery` now has those eight bodies, read from the JPL Small-Body Database on 2026-11-26, and that entry is about 600 bytes larger gzipped, which the size baseline records. Pluto stays on `factSheet`. Placing the `/asteroids` bodies in the space view was already on main. Chiron's Japanese name is キロン and Pholus's is フォルス.

- **The styled-orbit helpers run without a browser** (MODULES-1328). `MAX_PATHS`, `pathSpanMs`, `BUILT_IN_PATH_STYLES`, `completeStyle`, `styleTable` and `nearestPath` are on `/space` as well as the element. The guide's sample that calls `setPathStyles` is a page sample and is not run.

- **A page can draw a few orbits in looks of its own** (MODULES-1328). `setPaths` (the property `paths`) draws up to 16, each `{ id, style }`: a planet's, dwarf planet's or minor body's trip round the Sun, or a moon's trip round its planet. `setPathStyles` (the property `pathStyles`) changes the looks by name. The built-in looks `selected`, `hovered`, `favourite` and `path` differ by width and dash as well as colour. The lines are one overlay, drawn when the view draws and not while it rests, and they add no graphics-card draw. `path-hit` (8 px by default) makes `wakusei-path-hover` and `wakusei-path-click`; a body under the pointer is heard first. `pathAt` asks for any point. `stats.paths` counts drawn, dropped and unresolved.

- **Each belt carries its inner and outer orbit size** (MODULES-1350). `BELTS[id].innerAu` and `outerAu` are semi-major axes, the same numbers the English and Japanese sentences state (the Hildas are 3.9 to 4.1 au). The Trojans are 5.2 au at both edges, on Jupiter's orbit. The scattered belt's 2687 au is the semi-major axis of the farthest body carried (2014 FE72), not a distance from the Sun and not a sharp edge. `BELT_TOTALS.all` counts every asteroid, not the sum of the seven belts. NOTICE names the source of each pair.

- **The arrival test's waits have 60 seconds, and the test runs with slow frames** (MODULES-1588). `e2e/arrival.demo.ts` used the 5 second default of a poll; at a host load of 45 the frame a poll waited for was seconds away, once in five runs. With every frame 6.5 s after it was asked for the test failed on that 5 s before the change, and passes after; it now also runs with every frame 1.5 s late. Tests only.

- **The demo no longer zooms on a quick double tap on an iPhone** (MODULES-1827). kazoku-ui 0.54.5's page frame sets `touch-action: manipulation` on the document; the dependency is `^0.54.5`. `e2e/credits.demo.ts` reads it on the demo's pages. The package itself is unchanged.

Tests only: the package is unchanged by this release.

Beside the renderer's own frame, the page's family help script asks for one frame (a MutationObserver) to refresh tooltips when the DOM changes; the test already waits for it to run. The package asks for one frame at a time.

## [0.77.1] - 2026-11-26

- **A body's still is made at `still-size`, in a page that installs the package** (MODULES-1834, MODULES-1843). The package depended on kazoku-ui `^0.41.0`, and its own repo carried a local patch of that version so that the shared renderer made a still at the element's `data-still-size`; a patch is not published, so a page that installed wakusei-planets got the unpatched renderer and a body drawn 1,593 px wide was a 160 px picture blown up. The dependency is now `^0.54.4`, whose renderer reads `data-still-size` from the stage's host or the element round its shadow root, and the patch is gone. The still is as sharp as `still-size` times the device pixel ratio says (up to 2048 px on the long side).
- **Tests follow kazoku-ui 0.54.** `e2e/freeze.demo.ts` "a moving drag of a body draws at most thirty frames a second" counted animation frames asked for; since kazoku-ui 0.54 the scheduler asks again for a frame it deferred to hold 30 draws in any second, so it asked for 74 where 0.41 asked for 61 while drawing no more. It now counts the frames the renderer drew (`stats.renderer.frames.draws`), with the same limit; with the cap taken out of the scheduler and every draw asking for the next frame it counts 151 and fails. `e2e/kids.demo.ts` and the Japanese kids-sheets guide say `なまえ：` with the full-width colon, as the shared print part now writes it.

## [0.77.0] - 2026-11-25

- **`space.pathOf` gives a body's path over a stretch of time** (MODULES-1790). `pathOf(id, from, to, { steps, about })` returns the body's places on the screen from `from` to `to` (`steps` + 1 points, 96 by default, 1 to 512), each `{ ms, x, y, behind }` in CSS pixels in the element as `placeOf` has them, in the view's own scale (squeezed or true) and from the camera as it is now, so a page can draw a track that lies in the picture. A moon's path is taken round its planet by default (`about`), so the Moon's fortnight is a ring round the Earth; `about: null` is the line the body itself travels. It is worked out when asked from the last draw; nothing is drawn or stored for it. The Sun, the planets, the Moon, the other major bodies and the moons of the planets are placed; any other name gives an empty list. `BodyPathPoint` is exported with `BodyPlace`. The demo's space page has a Path row (a fortnight or a month, on the body in the middle). Tests: `test/body-path.test.ts` (the Moon's offset over the fortnight sweeps the 191.8 degrees an independent ephemeris gives), `e2e/body-path.demo.ts` (the path's middle is where the view draws the Moon at five moments, and a sidereal month closes the ring).

## [0.76.0] - 2026-11-24

- **Ganymede, Callisto and Io have their poles** (MODULES-1821). Their pictures were the USGS Mercator sheets between 57 degrees north and south, with the poles faded into a band. They are now made from the USGS global mosaics of Voyager and Galileo images, with the poles (Ganymede: the grey 1 km mosaic for the relief and the colour mosaic for the colour; Callisto: the 1 km mosaic; Io: the colour merge at 1 km). Ground that was never photographed, or only at low resolution, is painted to continue the photographed ground, and the credit says so.
- **Charon's south and Triton's north are painted ground** (MODULES-1821). New Horizons saw one hemisphere of Charon and Voyager 2 the south of Triton; the rest was flat grey. It is now painted from each moon's own photographed ground (Charon from the USGS New Horizons mosaic, Triton from the Voyager 2 colour mosaic), with the red cap of Charon's north and Triton's south polar cap and its streaks kept where they were photographed.
- **Ariel has a picture** (MODULES-1821). It had none. Its picture is the Voyager 2 mosaic of its south, with the north, which was never photographed, painted to continue it.

## [0.75.2] - 2026-11-24

- **Arrokoth is a body** (MODULES-558). `arrokoth` is a minor body: the shape and the picture of NASA's published model (New Horizons; public domain, credit NASA Visualization Technology Applications and Development), drawn as that mesh rather than a map wrapped on a globe. It is a contact binary. The orbit is the Small-Body Database ellipse at JD 2461200.5, the elements the asteroid list used, not a fitted table. Arrokoth is no longer a row of `/asteroids` (101 remain). The mesh is its own file and the entry points that reach the minor bodies grow by about 114 KB gzipped, which the size baseline records. The picture on each of these meshes is given back when the view that loaded it is removed. The `/asteroids` examples and guide use Itokawa, which is still a row of that list.
- **Bennu is a body** (MODULES-558). `bennu` is a minor body: the shape and the colour picture of NASA's published model (OSIRIS-REx; public domain, credit NASA Visualization Technology Applications and Development), drawn as that mesh rather than a map wrapped on a globe. The orbit is the Horizons ellipse at JD 2461200.5, the elements the asteroid list used, not a fitted table. Bennu is no longer a row of `/asteroids` (102 remain). The mesh is its own file and the entry points that reach the minor bodies grow by about 56 KB gzipped, which the size baseline records.
- **Eros is a body** (MODULES-558). `eros` is a minor body: the shape and the colour picture of NASA's published model (NEAR Shoemaker; public domain, credit NASA Visualization Technology Applications and Development), drawn as that mesh rather than a map wrapped on a globe. The orbit is the Small-Body Database ellipse at JD 2461200.5, the elements the asteroid list used, not a fitted table. Eros is no longer a row of `/asteroids` (103 remain). The mesh is its own file and the entry points that reach the minor bodies grow by about 34 KB gzipped, which the size baseline records. Bodies with no spacecraft picture are unchanged. The Japanese looks guide names Arrokoth the same way as the other bodies: the code id, then the Japanese name. That corrects the sentence the Kuiper-belt wording had just written.

- **Ceres's radius matches JPL's extent** (MODULES-1761). The drawn radius was 481.05 km and the flattening 0.0731. JPL's Small-Body Database, read 2026-11-23, gives the extent 964.4 x 964.2 x 891.8 km (Park et al. 2016, sigma 0.2 km). The radius is now 482.15 km, the mean of the equatorial axes, and the flattening 0.075184, which puts the pole at 445.9 km. The 966.2 x 962.0 x 891.8 km figure is not used: the Ermakov et al. 2017 page did not open, and that paper's abstract gives about 470 km, the volume-equivalent size (the database's diameter 939.4 km). The pole and the spin are unchanged. MODULES-1484 had changed the citation only.

- **Every demo page says what it is and credits its data** (MODULES-1764). The build writes kazoku-ui's page header and credit line where the page marks `<!--frame:header-->` and `<!--frame:credits-->`. An embed has the credit line only. The family's checker is kazoku-ui's of 2026-11-11b. Tests: `test/page-frame.test.ts`, `test/standard.test.ts`.
- **Pluto's south and soft far side are painted ground** (MODULES-1798). New Horizons never photographed the south of Pluto, about 30 degrees south and beyond, and saw the far side only from a long way off, where the map is a smear; the map made the south one plain tone. The three pictures (`pluto-512`, `-1024`, `-2048`) now show sharp ground everywhere: where New Horizons saw it sharply (the close-up hemisphere, the north polar cap) it is NASA's 300 m LORRI/MVIC mosaic coloured by the Ralph/MVIC map (public domain), unchanged; the south and the soft far side are painted with an image model to continue it, keeping the far side's observed broad pattern of dark and bright ground, matched in tone and contrast to the photographed ground at the edge and joined over a few hundred pixels, so a globe shows no dark half, no smear and no seam. South of about 30 degrees S was not photographed by New Horizons, and the far side only at low resolution; those parts are painted with an image model to continue the photographed ground, keeping the far side's observed broad pattern, and are not observed in detail. The credit is in `NOTICE.md` and in `PICTURE_CREDITS` (`changes` of the Pluto row). `pluto-512` and `-1024` are 29.8 and 84.5 KB (were 12.9 and 45.7), still inside the 2.3 MB budget; `pluto-2048` is 346.9 KB (was 183.7). `scripts/make-textures.py` no longer builds Pluto: the 16384 by 8192 master is kept offline. The package's picture budget goes from 2.3 to 2.4 MB for the larger Pluto maps.
- **Bennu is drawn when the view goes to it.** The camera's near plane had a floor of 0.0005 (about 3 km), and the view goes to Bennu to 1 km, so its middle was nearer than the plane and it was neither drawn nor placed (`placeOf` gave `null`). The floor is now a tenth of the distance when the camera is nearer than 32 km. The embed page's credit line no longer takes room from the frame in the flex row, and the tests that count the bodies of the view count Eros, Bennu and Arrokoth.

## [0.75.1] - 2026-11-23

- **The Jupiter chart's phone test waits for the redraw** (MODULES-1802). Narrowing the page paints the chart again. The test reads a label's box only after that paint, and the box still has to be at least 11 pixels tall.

- **A body's name is drawn whole, in front of the body** (MODULES-1797). On a lumpy moon (Thebe, Amalthea and the like) the name is a band on a sphere of radius 1, and the shape stands out past that sphere, so letters sat inside the surface. The band is now written on the shell that clears the shape's highest point (`stats.label.shell`: 1 on a smooth globe, more on a lumpy one). On a smooth globe the same band turns with the body, so a long name such as Jupiter's runs off the limb and the planet cuts the letters. The name now slides about the pole, only as far as it needs, until every letter is on the near side; a name already on the front still turns with the body, and `body-label-facing` is unchanged. `e2e/name-shell.demo.ts` checks the shell.

- **`<wakusei-space>` says which body the camera is at after `turnTo`** (MODULES-1352). `turnTo(id)` turns the view where the camera stands, but `body` then named the turned-to body, so a page could not tell "looking at" from "at". `body` is unchanged (the body looked at); the new `at` is the body the camera stands at, equal to `body` except between a `turnTo` and the next move of the camera (`goTo`, setting `body`, a hop). `turnTo` has always declined to turn a riding view; the docs now say so beside the Moon. `e2e/space-turn.demo.ts` checks both.

- **Pluto's day and night meet in a soft fade** (MODULES-1798). The haze stayed bright a little way onto the night side, so a lighter strip sat beside the line. The extra haze now fades out at the terminator. It is drawn at `atmosphere.strength` (`atmosphereTint`). Every body but Pluto has strength 1, which is the limb it already had; Pluto stays at 0.12, and its terminator wraps further (`wrap` 0.55).
- **Styx and Kerberos are drawn with Pluto's other moons** (MODULES-1798). The catalogue had them, with `full` off, so a row of the moons drawn in full listed Charon, Nix and Hydra. They are now spheres of the catalogue's radii (5.2 km and 6 km), placed by the catalogue's mean elements (PLU060, epoch 2026-01-01). No measured shape or spin, so they do not turn. Pluto's flat map marks five moons.

- **Wikipedia is no longer a data source** (MODULES-1762). The 356 irregular-moon magnitudes that were read from English Wikipedia (CC BY-SA) now come from the V(1,0) column of the U.S. Naval Observatory's satellite table, where that column prints a number (24 moons). The other 332 have no magnitude and no estimated size. Japanese names cite the National Museum of Nature and Science's moon table, and a spelling that table writes differently is the one kept. Hippocamp and Valetudo have no name in that table, so they show their English names. Styx and Kerberos are drawn, so they keep the package's own Japanese names, and the all-moons poster names them without the estimated star. The star note comes from Himalia, which is still estimated. The dwarf-planet sizes already named their papers.

## [0.75.0] - 2026-11-23

- **The moons on their planet's flat map** (MODULES-1787, part of MODULES-1785). `<wakusei-flat moons>` marks each moon of the body over the place on its ground that it is straight above: Earth's Moon, Mars's two, Jupiter's six, Saturn's nineteen, Uranus's five, Neptune's four, Pluto's three (`moonsOfBody`, `flatMoons`). Each moon is a named dot, pressable (`wakusei-flat-pick`, `kind: "mark"`, `mark.group` `moons`, `mark.data` `{ moon, distanceKm }`), reached by the arrow keys, read aloud and in the key. Off until `moons` (or the new **Moons** chip) asks; `moons="io,europa"` chooses some. With `track`, or by choosing a moon, the path each moon draws over the ground is drawn, half a turn of the planet either way (`flatMoonTracks`, `flatMoonTrackHalf`). The point is worked out from the planet's pole and prime meridian and the moon's place from the package's own theories; the unit test holds the Moon's point to its phase (the angle from the Sun's point is 180 degrees less the phase angle, to 0.5 degree). The landers and rovers were already on a body's map as places, and the orbiters are the Moon's and Mars's (the package holds no others); `e2e/flat-moons.demo.ts` and `test/flat-moons.test.ts` are the tests. The demo's flat page has the chip and `?moons=1`.

- **`<wakusei-flat>` draws any planet or moon, as a photo or a line map** (MODULES-1786, part of MODULES-1785). `body` takes any of 28 bodies the package has a picture of: Mercury, Venus, Earth, the Moon, Mars, Phobos, Deimos, Ceres, Vesta, Jupiter and its four big moons, Saturn and its moons (Mimas to Phoebe, Titan), Uranus, Neptune, Triton, Pluto and Charon (`FLAT_BODIES`, `isFlatBody`). The Moon and Mars are as before; the others have no orbiters, so their footprint, ground-track and orbiter buttons are left out, and the Earth, Uranus, Neptune, Phobos, Deimos and Phoebe have no places in the package.
  - **Two looks.** `look="photo"` (the default, what the element has always drawn) is the body's own picture; `look="map"` is a line map drawn from the same picture on the reader's machine: its brightness smoothed and cut into five dark tones of equal area, a pale line where one tone meets the next, a grid every 30 degrees (15, then 10 as the zoom goes in) with its degrees along the edges, and the named features marked. A planet has no outline data, so the lines are the picture's albedo contours. The tones are made once for each picture and freed when the look goes back. The Map / Photo buttons are `controls="... look"` (not among the defaults, so no page gets a new button); `look` and `lookState` (`loading`, `map`, `photo`, `failed`) are properties and `wakusei-flat-look` carries `{ look, state }`. A page that does not ask for the map changes in nothing. (chikyu-earth's `<chikyu-flat>` starts on its drawn map; here the picture is the existing look, so it stays the default.)
  - **The night and the Sun.** The night side is drawn plainly, clearly darker, with a pale dashed line along the sunrise and sunset line (the circle where the Sun is on the horizon; `sunriseLine`) and the Sun's dot with a pale ring where it is overhead, for the time shown and for any body (`flatSun`, `flatSunRate`). Each body has its own twilight: 1 degree where there is no air, 8 on Mars, 10 on Venus and Titan, 12 on Earth, a few on the giants. `shade="off"` turns all three off.
  - **The key, the credit and the words.** A key under the map (`controls` word `key`, on by default, folded shut on a phone; `keyOpen`) names every mark drawn now with a small picture of it, in English and Japanese, and holds the picture's whole credit. The credit is one small line at the map's bottom edge in both looks (`Mars: NASA / USGS (public domain)`, shorter on a phone, a tap shows all of it). Same words and same shape as chikyu-earth's flat map (`look`, `key`, `lookGroup`, `lookMap`, `lookPhoto`, `keyShow`, `keyHide`, `keyMarks`).
  - **A way in for marks of your own** (for the moons, favourites, orbiters and landers of the tickets that follow). `setMarks(group, marks, label)`, `clearMarks(group?)`, `marks` and `groups`: a mark is `{ id, lat, lon, name, group?, shape?, colour?, size?, labelled?, data? }` with the shapes `dot`, `ring`, `diamond`, `square`, `cross` and `star`. Marks are drawn over the picture, named on hover, tap and focus, reached by the arrow keys, read aloud, listed in the key and sent in `wakusei-flat-pick` with `kind: "mark"` and the record (`mark`). Pure side: `readMarks`, `markName`, `MARK_SHAPES`.
  - **Pure parts** in `/flat`: `FLAT_LOOKS`, `readLook`, `toneMap`, `toneRamp`, `bandEdges`, `pictureWidth`, `pictureWidths` (Europa has only the 1024 picture), `creditWho`, `creditLicence`, `sunriseLine`, `flatSunRate`, `gridText` and the types `FlatLook`, `FlatMark`, `HeldMark`, `MarkShape`, with unit tests (`test/flat-look.test.ts`: every body has its picture, credit row and a Sun; the Sun on the Earth at the solstices; the sunrise line 90 degrees from the Sun on six bodies; the picture's projection; the tones) and browser tests (`e2e/flat-look.demo.ts`, desktop and phone, Chromium and WebKit). The demo's flat page has rows for the planets and the moons and the Map and Photo buttons (`?look=map`). No new picture or data file: the line map is made from the pictures the 3D views already use.
  - **Size.** `./flat` is 2.8 kB gzipped larger (53,161 B; the new words in both languages, the tones, the marks and the sunrise line) and its baseline is moved by that; the 28 bodies' credits are not in it (`creditWho` and `creditLicence` hold a few words each).

- **`still-size` makes a still sharp** (MODULES-1795). A `<wakusei-body live="off">` still was always made at 160 pixels on its long side and scaled up by the page, so a large still was blurred; the `data-still-size` a page set was read by nothing. The element now takes `still-size` (CSS pixels on the long side) and keeps `data-still-size` on itself at that times the device pixel ratio (up to 3, at most 2048), which the shared renderer reads; changing it makes the still again. An element with no `still-size` is as before. `e2e/still-size.demo.ts` checks the size of the picture made.

## 0.11.1 - 2026-11-03

The dwarf planets' realistic look is smooth when it is small (MODULES-332).

- **No more pixel-sized specks on a small body.** At about 80 px (the portrait builder) Ceres, Orcus, Quaoar, Makemake, Gonggong and Sedna showed square dark specks: the craters and the fine noise were finer than a pixel and were sampled anyway. ## 0.5.0 - 2026-11-03

The bodies for someone else's three.js scene.
