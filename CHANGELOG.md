# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

- **Pluto's unseen south is a plain tone with a soft edge, not a dark half** (MODULES-993). New Horizons never saw the south in daylight, and the map made it a dark brown that began on one straight line about 7 degrees south of the equator, so a globe showed a hard-edged dark half and the name written on the equator ran into it. The never-imaged part is now one plain tone, the mapped ground's average times 0.85, and the mapped detail fades into it over 8 percent of the map's height along an edge that follows the real coverage (about 30 degrees south), so the equator, and a name on it, are all on mapped ground. The three maps (512, 1024 and 2048) are 12.6, 44.6 and 179.4 KB, up from 10.0, 36.2 and 147.5 KB (+43.9 KB in all). Picture test `e2e/map-gaps.demo.ts` reads the shipped map: the detail lost over 1.5 percent of its height is 0.59 to 0.64 of the most before and 0.16 to 0.19 after; the unseen part's brightness is 0.26 of the mapped ground's before and 0.75 after. The finer map tiles of Pluto are made from this picture and need making again (`pnpm tiles`).

- **A floating name is no longer cut off at the view's edge** (MODULES-1144). A name beside a body near the right edge ran past the edge and was cut. It now goes to the dot's left side (the dot follows the text), and if it fits on neither side, or would pass the top or the bottom, it is held inside the edge; a body whose dot is outside the view keeps its name where it falls. `nameSpot(dot, size, frame)` from `/space` is the placing on plain numbers. `e2e/space-names.demo.ts` reads 3 names cut at three view sizes on the old code, none now.

## [0.40.0] - 2026-11-13

- **Paint an orbiter's path on the Moon or Mars** (MODULES-1168). `space.paint = { id, length, swath }` on `<wakusei-space>` paints that craft's ground track on the body it goes round (`id` such as `lro` or `mro`; `length` `1d` by default, or `1w`, `1m`, in the clock's time; `swath` `track` by default, or `coverage`, the ground where the craft is at least 10° up). `clearPaint()` takes it off, and another craft clears the old trail. Parts older than `length` fade and are dropped. The line is filled between frames, including backwards. One upload a frame while time moves, nothing at rest, and nothing while paint is off, when the picture and its sphere are freed on the graphics card. The craft's place is sampled at most a third of a trip apart, so a fast clock follows the orbit; a clock run backwards lays down the older end; a `coverage` swath over the date line is painted on both edges. The date-line split, the pole split and the swath radius are `@johnmorrisdotca/wakusei-planets/ground-track` (no DOM, no three.js). The space page has Paint, Clear, and 1 day, 1 week, 1 month.
- **Ride Off puts the demo's clock back** (the space page). A ride along starts the clock, and Off used to leave it running, at 3,600 times real time, with the view drawing every frame. Off now holds the clock again when the ride started it, and leaves it running at its own speed when it was already playing. Demo only.
- **The freeze test of the space view holds the clock** (MODULES-1187). "Changes to the space view give back what they took" read 183 graphics objects against a limit of 182 in the image: the rides in its round started the demo's clock, so the moons came and went with the time and the count was of a different moment each round. The round now holds the clock after the rides and waits for 30 frames with no draw before it counts. Test only.
- **The travel test watches a move on the page** (MODULES-1186). `e2e/space-travel.demo.ts` counted the draws of a move from the test, one round trip for each reading, so a loaded machine saw 6 frames of a move it needs more than 6 of. It now records each new draw at every animation frame on the page until the move ends. Test only.

- **Orbiters: the ground beneath, the next pass over a place, and the inclination to the body's equator** (MODULES-929). `orbiterGround(id, when)` and `orbiterGroundTrack(id, from, to, points)` give the point and the line under a craft (planetocentric latitude, east longitude, from the body's IAU pole and prime meridian); `orbiterNextPass(id, place, when, { minElevationDeg, withinDays })` its next pass over a place on the body: rise, highest moment and elevation, set and status (`OrbiterPass`). New entry `@johnmorrisdotca/wakusei-planets/belts` (pure: no page, no three.js): 3,340 numbered small bodies from NASA/JPL's Small-Body Database in seven belts (`BELT_IDS`: the main belt, the Hildas, the Jupiter Trojans, the Centaurs, and the Kuiper belt as classical, resonant and scattered objects), each carrying its `belt`; `loadBeltBodies()` fetches them (a chunk of their own, about 70 kB gzipped); `beltPositions`, `beltCounts`, `beltDensity` (how many, and to the cubic au, by distance from the Sun), `beltSpacing` (how far apart the bodies of a belt really are: about 4 million km, ten Moon distances, in the main belt even with its 1.4 million known bodies) and `beltNote` (the sentence the view shows); `BELTS` and `BELT_TOTALS` say what each belt is and how many the database holds, against the 1,580,096 known asteroids and 8,338 known trans-Neptunian objects of 2026-11-10; `OORT` is the Oort cloud's edges; `beltsSource()` is the belts as a source of the data door. - **The camera travels from one body to the next along a path of its own** (MODULES-912). Before, pressing a body glided the camera in a straight line from where it was, so the move could swing sideways through empty space or lose both bodies from the picture. `goTo` and `ride` now move the camera along `travelPose` (from `/space`, a pure function of time, with no clock and no DOM; `travelPlan` gives its stages, a suggested length and its easing). The default move, `turn-travel`, turns (or backs up) beside the body being left until the destination is in the picture, goes straight at it with it in view and growing, and settles into the framed view. The look direction turns one way only, and one of the two bodies is in the picture at every moment. For two bodies of one planet's family (two of its moons, or the planet and a moon) the camera circles the planet, which stays in the picture, and a turn that would carry the camera away from a near moon at once begins as a turn about that moon. The path keeps `TRAVEL_CLEARANCE` radii above the surface of the Sun, the Earth and the Moon, the planets, the dwarf planets and the other small bodies of the view; the moons of the other planets are not looked for. Every body is taken where the clock puts it as the move starts and as it ends (a clock at an hour a second moves the Moon about three Earth radii in a five-second move), and the last view is framed for the destination where it will be. `transition` (an attribute, a property and an option of each call) takes `turn-travel` (the default), `direct`, `look-back`, `auto` and `cut`; `auto` is `direct` when the destination is on the screen as the move starts. `pace` (an attribute and property) multiplies the plan's length and `duration` (an option, in milliseconds) sets one move's length. ## 0.1.0 - 2026-11-03

The first release. The package, to the family's standard from the start: a demo and an embed page on GitHub Pages, a documentation site in English and Japanese, a README with every way to use it (each one tested), the shared release workflow with its freeze test.

- Line drawings, as SVG strings and as `<wakusei-body look="line">`, of the Sun, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn (with its rings), Uranus (with its rings), Neptune and Pluto, and of the Moon, Phobos and Deimos, the Galilean moons, Saturn's seven larger moons, Uranus's five, Neptune's Proteus, Triton and Nereid, and Pluto's Charon, Nix and Hydra: real relative sizes, axial tilt and rotation as data.
- The Earth, through a thin adapter over chikyu-earth's own globe (`/earth`, chikyu-earth an optional peer): its line globe, and its Earth from space when the body is live. No second copy.
- Shaded realistic drawings with three.js through kazoku-ui's shared renderer, each body its own optional entry point: bands, spots, craters, caps and the Pluto heart worked out in a shader, rings that cast shadows, the Sun's glow.
- The sky for a date and time: a body's pole, rotation, phase and ring opening as the Earth sees it, and a planet's moons on their orbits where they are then (JPL elements, Meeus for the Moon), worked out about once a second and carried forward between.
- One WebGL context per page, nothing drawn at rest, thirty frames a second at most, canvas within 4096 pixels a side and 6 MP, everything freed, no drawing out of sight, reduced motion respected, a lost context recovers; tested by `pnpm test:freeze` and a soak.
